Taxi Demand & Fleet Utilization Analysis: Blue Bird

Analysis of Blue Bird taxi requests and driver shifts to find why drivers sit idle while customers go unserved, ending in KPI-based recommendations.

Context / Problem
  • Blue Bird runs a conventional fleet with permanent drivers, so every hour a car sits empty is cost without revenue.
  • At the same time some customers could not get a taxi, and corporate clients questioned how eVouchers were being used.
  • Goal: find out why many drivers sit idle while some requests go unfulfilled, using six MySQL tables from October to December 2023 (pair project).
Key Findings
  • Failed requests cluster in the CBD at peak hours: 36.6% failed at 07:00–08:59 and 17:00–18:59, against 5.5% at other hours. Those four hours hold 45.6% of all failures.
  • Drivers are very idle: 1.26 trips per shift on average and 30.6% of shifts with no trips at all.
  • Location matters more than pool: the Airport has 21.8% of shifts but only 12.6% of requests, and 51% of Airport shifts had no trips.
  • 65.9% of Airport eVoucher trips happen at night or on weekends, against 22.6% for other trips. This is a pattern to verify, not proof of misuse.
Impact / Recommendations
  • Pilot shift scheduling that puts more drivers in the CBD during the two peak windows, measured against the 36.6% failure rate.
  • Trial moving some Airport shifts to the CBD at peak hours to cut empty shifts.
  • Verify weekend and night eVoucher trips against client travel policies before drawing conclusions.
  • Track trips per shift weekly by zone so management can see whether changes work.

Indonesia Well-being Statistical Analysis: World Happiness Report

Statistical study of Indonesia’s life evaluation scores in the World Happiness Report: trends, ASEAN comparison and factors linked to happiness.

Context / Problem
  • How happy is Indonesia, how has it changed, and how does it compare with other countries?
  • Using World Happiness Report data, I studied Indonesia's life evaluation scores for 2021–2025 against the global average and ASEAN peers.
Key Findings
  • Indonesia's life evaluation stayed between 5.24 and 5.62 (average 5.46), with a gradual upward trend and no outliers.
  • The factors that move most closely with happiness are freedom to make life choices (r = 0.89), generosity (r = −0.88) and social support (r = 0.79). These are correlations, not causes.
  • Indonesia ranked 5th of the 7 ASEAN countries with data, slightly above the global average in 2023–2024 but below it in other years.
Impact / Recommendations
  • Points to where to look first: freedom of choice and social support are the areas most worth deeper study for well-being programs.
  • Shows that conclusions need a longer time range and inferential testing before guiding policy decisions.

Blue Bird Operations Monitoring Dashboard

Tableau monitoring dashboard that continues the Blue Bird analysis, tracking revenue, trips, fleet productivity and demand in one place.

Context / Problem
  • Purpose: dashboard monitoring. It keeps the Blue Bird findings visible so the business can watch the same numbers every day, not only in a one-off analysis.
  • Users: the COO for the executive overview, fleet and pool managers for operational performance, and the commercial and planning team for demand and pricing.
  • Problem: drivers sit idle while some customers cannot get a taxi, and nobody sees early when a pool, service type or hour slips.
Key Findings
  • Must-know metrics for the COO: total revenue (Rp 25.64B), total trips (307.7K), average revenue per trip (Rp 83.3K), revenue per driver (Rp 6.32M) and charter share of revenue (15.1%), each with change vs the previous month.
  • For fleet managers: trips per shift (fleet average 1.23), trips per shift by pool, shift window, employment type and service type, and the spread of trips per driver. Pool Bandara Soetta is the lowest at 1.02.
  • For demand planning: the 3 busiest hours take 37.9% of trips, with the peak around 12:00 to 16:00, plus revenue and average fare by hour and by service type.
  • Service mix: Bluebird has 62.1% of trips but 56.8% of revenue, while Goldenbird has 7.1% of trips but 10.4% of revenue.
Impact / Recommendations
  • Spots weak pools, hours and service types early, so fleet allocation and shift scheduling can be adjusted before revenue slips.
  • Month, Service Type and Pool filters let each user check their own area without a separate report.
  • Next step: add alert thresholds for trips per shift and revenue per driver.

NYC Green Taxi Carbon Emissions and EV Charging Gap

Data storytelling deck on NYC green taxi carbon emissions and the EV charging gap, turning one month of trip data into a prioritized charger plan.

Context / Problem
  • Purpose: turn the emissions analysis into an executive story (Situation, Complication, Resolution) that ends with a clear decision.
  • Questions: where do taxis emit the most carbon, are chargers where they are needed, and where should new stations go first.
  • Data: NYC green taxi trips for January 2023 (68,211 rows, 54,189 after cleaning), 846 EV stations in NYC and zone boundaries.
Key Findings
  • Manhattan produces 54% of the 46.8 tons of CO₂ (Jan 2023), about 7.3× the average zone intensity. Queens follows with 29.3% and Brooklyn with 12.9%.
  • Four uptown zones (East Harlem North and South, Central Harlem, Morningside Heights) give about 18.8 tons, around 40% of city emissions, but have only about 2 to 6 EV stations each. Upper East Side has up to 22.
  • Manhattan emissions peak from 07:00 to 20:00 on weekdays and fall to 33 kg per hour or less in the early morning.
  • Queens has 29.3% of emissions but only 18.4% of stations.
Impact / Recommendations
  • Three steps: build fast charging in the four uptown zones, offer cheaper night charging from 01:00 to 06:00, then expand to Queens and southern Brooklyn.
  • Scenario: electrifying the four zones could cut up to 72.9% of Manhattan pickup emissions. This is an upper bound (full conversion, tailpipe emissions only).
  • Limits: modeled emissions, one month of data, green taxis only, and station count is not charging capacity.

Employee Data Management & Validation System

Python console application for managing employee records with create, read, update and delete, plus input validation, a recycle bin and an update log.

Context / Problem
  • HR teams need employee records that stay accurate and can be recovered when mistakes happen.
Key Findings
  • Validation blocks bad data at entry: dates, emails, phone and bank numbers, salary and duplicate national IDs.
  • Employee IDs are generated per department (for example FIN001 and IT004).
  • Deleted records go to a recycle bin, and every update is logged with old and new values.
  • A statistics view reports headcount, salary totals and averages, and tenure.
Impact / Recommendations
  • Clean data at the source means HR reports and analysis can be trusted without heavy cleaning later.
  • The update log gives an audit trail, so changes to salaries or roles can be checked.
  • Next step: save data to a database so it persists after the program closes.
